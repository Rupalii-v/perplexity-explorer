07 / AI CONCEPTS & FOUNDATIONS

Theoretical Foundations

Understand the core mathematical and algorithmic concepts powering autoregressive language models, from tokenization to cross-entropy and perplexity.

01 What is a Language Model?
↓

A statistical or neural model that assigns a joint probability distribution over sequences of words or tokens.

P(w_1, w_2, ..., w_N)
Generative models learn to predict the most probable continuations given a prompt.
Autoregressive models factorize sequence probability into the sequential product of individual conditional probabilities.
02 What is a Token?
↓

The atomic unit of text processed by a neural model. A token can be a full word, a subword, a single character, or punctuation.

Token ∈ {0, 1, ..., |V| - 1}
"Python" → Token ID 12608 in GPT-2's vocabulary.
Tokens bridge raw continuous human writing into discrete integer embeddings that neural network matrix operations can consume.
03 What is Tokenization?
↓

The algorithmic process of segmenting continuous strings into a sequence of discrete token IDs.

f: String → [id_1, id_2, ..., id_k]
GPT-2 utilizes Byte-Level Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), ensuring zero out-of-vocabulary errors.
Byte-level BPE iteratively merges the most frequent byte pairs in training data until a fixed vocabulary size is reached.
04 What are Logits?
↓

The unnormalized raw real-valued numbers produced by the final linear projection layer of the transformer.

z = W_vocab × h_final + b ∈ ℝ^{|V|}
Higher logits indicate stronger raw affinity before normalisation into probabilities.
Logits are unbounded: they can be positive, negative, or zero, and do not sum to 1 until converted by softmax.
05 What is Softmax?
↓

A non-linear activation function converting unbounded logits into a normalized probability distribution summing strictly to 1.

P(token_i) = exp(logit_i) / Σ_j exp(logit_j)
Exponentiates all logits and normalizes by their collective sum.
Numerical stability is achieved by computing exp(logit - max(logits)), preventing numerical overflow.
06 What is Conditional Probability?
↓

The likelihood of observing an outcome given that prior events or context tokens have already occurred.

P(w_t | w_1, w_2, ..., w_{t-1})
P("Paris" | "The capital of France is") ≫ P("Paris" | "The capital of Spain is").
Causal self-attention enforces that each token position can only attend to earlier tokens, preserving causal conditioning.
07 What is Next-Token Prediction?
↓

The pretraining objective of causal language models: given tokens 1 through t, maximize probability of token t+1.

argmax_w P(w | prompt)
Generation repeats this step iteratively, appending newly sampled tokens to the prompt.
Sampling methods include Greedy (argmax), Temperature scaling, Top-K filtering, and Nucleus (Top-P) truncation.
08 What is Cross-Entropy?
↓

The standard loss function measuring information divergence between true token targets and predicted probability distributions.

H(p, q) = - Σ p(x) ln q(x) = - ln P(target_token)
Minimizing cross-entropy is mathematically equivalent to maximizing log-likelihood.
Cross-entropy measures the average number of nats/bits needed to identify the true token using the model's distribution.
09 What is Perplexity?
↓

The exponential of the average cross-entropy loss over a sequence. Interpretable as the model's effective branching factor.

PP = exp( - 1/N Σ ln P_i )
A perplexity of 20 means the model is as confused as if choosing uniformly among 20 words.
Perplexity normalizes for sequence length N, enabling fair comparisons across sentences of varying durations.
10 Why Low or High Perplexity?
↓

High perplexity stems from grammatical anomalies, rare words, out-of-domain patterns, or unexpected phrasing.

↓ PP ⇔ Expected; ↑ PP ⇔ Surprising
"I love Python" has low perplexity; "Python love I" produces high perplexity.
Because perplexity is model-dependent, a human-grammatical sentence can still exhibit high perplexity if unrepresented in training.

About Perplexity Explorer

Perplexity Explorer is an open-source, interactive miniature AI research laboratory designed to illuminate the inner mathematical mechanics of autoregressive causal language models.

Unlike high-level chat interfaces that hide probabilities, this explorer executes an actual 82M-parameter model (DistilGPT-2) directly inside your web browser via WebAssembly and Transformers.js.

Key Highlights:

  • Zero fabricated probabilities — every probability originates from real model output logits.
  • Numerically stable log-softmax and log-sum perplexity computations.
  • Byte-level BPE tokenizer dissection with whitespace detection.
  • Autoregressive continuation generator with greedy sampling.
  • Offline manual simulator for experimenting with mathematical edge cases.
Constructed with vanilla HTML, modern CSS, and plain JavaScript in a single, zero-build bundle.
